# HERMES research gap map

Reviewed 19 November 2026.

**The best immediate improvement project is to locate recoverable evidence loss and test a faithful causal scorer before choosing a new memory mechanism.** The reviewed literature already implements most broad ideas suggested for HERMES: adaptive hierarchy, semantic query banks, object memory, delayed-query summaries, selective historical retrieval and generation-time visual reinjection. The present experiments establish a scorer discrepancy and an inconclusive HERMES–FIFO accuracy difference; they do not demonstrate that a proposed replacement improves answers. A small controlled pilot can determine whether causal probe-state reuse deserves development, whether previous-answer conditioning harms later retention, or whether failures mainly originate in sampling and backbone capability. Those are research questions, not certified original contributions. This report preserves the broader questions in a searchable register so that choosing one direction does not erase the others.

## All 51 local files are accounted for at stated review depths

The [paper review ledger](<HERMES paper review ledger.csv>) contains one row for every PDF in the local collection, including supporting benchmarks, backbone reports, peripheral agent material and a historical psychology reprint. The inventory comprises **51 PDF files: 34 directly relevant, 13 supporting and four peripheral**, representing 1,261 PDF pages. Page count measures material accounted for, not pages read closely. The ACL and arXiv HERMES files describe the same underlying work in different versions; they are not two independent methods. Every recorded SHA-256 was checked against its actual source PDF, and the ledger filenames match the directory exactly. Review depth is retained per file, alongside contributions, mechanisms, evaluations, author statements, analyst inferences, candidate questions, overlaps and inexpensive tests. ([Local PDF collection](../related_papers/), [ledger](<HERMES paper review ledger.csv>))

The three detailed reviews cover 17 files each: [early corpus](<../research_notes/HERMES research gap map/corpus_early.md>), [middle corpus](<../research_notes/HERMES research gap map/corpus_middle.md>) and [recent corpus](<../research_notes/HERMES research gap map/corpus_recent.md>). They selectively inspected abstracts, methods, evaluation setups and limitation passages. Large supplements and peripheral sources received narrower reading. Four additional primary abstract pages were checked for LORE-KV, KV², Video-MemComp and DeltaS; these are separately labeled [online updates](<../research_notes/HERMES research gap map/online_updates.json>) and do not increase local PDF coverage. Their full papers and implementations were not independently audited in this pass. The earlier [novelty landscape](<../research_notes/HERMES journal publication strategy/novelty_landscape.md>) and [publication strategy](<HERMES journal publication strategy.md>) supply background; the newer source corrections below take precedence.

The [gap register](<HERMES gap register.csv>) consolidates distinct questions rather than treating each mention as a new gap. It distinguishes project implementation fixes, project evaluation gaps, partly addressed problems, already implemented directions and speculative candidates. An author-stated limitation is evidence about that paper and version; later work can address it. Conversely, a future-work sentence does not establish an implemented solution. Where no author limitation passage was verified, the ledger says so. This is a comprehensive accounting of the bounded review, **not an exhaustive proof of all gaps in the global literature**.

The measured project foundation is substantial but narrow. On the evaluated StreamingBench real-time visual track, HERMES 4K answers 1,555 of 2,495 questions correctly and FIFO 4K answers 1,546, across 498 videos. The difference is +0.3607 percentage points, with a 95% video-cluster bootstrap interval of −0.4810 to +1.1628. There are 58 HERMES-only successes, 49 FIFO-only successes and 891 shared failures. Episodic questions yield 579/997 for both methods. Equal task totals can hide different individual predictions. These observations leave a general advantage and equivalence unestablished for this 0.5B configuration. The full comparison also combines sessions and hardware; the matched efficiency study has a separate scope. ([Project status](../PROJECT_STATUS.md), [paired results](../../results/five_stage/full_benchmark_paired.csv))

## Existing methods close broad novelty claims and sharpen the remaining questions

**Prompt-guided importance and adaptive hierarchy are established mechanisms.** InfiniPot injects a Catalyst Prompt to estimate importance before unknown future questions, combined with novelty under compression (§3; limitations §6, PDF p9). HERMES assigns recency, semantic importance and anchors by layer and already includes smoothing and positional reindexing. SAVEMem uses a fixed five-question bank, semantic matching, three memory tiers, recency gating and selective forgetting (PDF pp4–5). FluxMem introduces scene-adaptive temporal/spatial compression, while CurveStream uses feature-trajectory curvature and adaptive thresholds. A new query bank, recent/old split, scene threshold or layer ratio alone would need a much more specific contribution. ([InfiniPot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01518), [HERMES](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.381/), [SAVEMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07897v1), [FluxMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02096v1), [CurveStream](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19571v1))

Several older author limitations are therefore **partly addressed**, not untouched opportunities. ReKV explicitly flags growing offloaded storage, fixed frame blocks and fixed retrieval count (PDF p16). StreamKV later adds semantic segmentation, summary retrieval and layer-adaptive compression; SAVEMem adapts retrieval scope. InfiniPot-V leaves fixed temporal-redundancy/value-norm allocation and unified multimodal compression open (Appendix H, PDF p24), but multiple adaptive visual-memory methods now exist. The remaining comparative question is which policy preserves useful evidence under the same total resource constraints, rather than whether adaptation can be introduced at all. ([ReKV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00540v1), [StreamKV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07278v1), [InfiniPot-V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15745v2))

**Broad EDMI-style visual reinjection has direct implemented overlap.** StreamFlow §3.4, titled “Attention-Guided Memory Injection,” monitors visual attention mass using VAS (equation 8) and injects historical visual latents during generation when visual grounding weakens; the description occupies local PDF pp5–6, with related architecture in §3.1. OASIS already performs selective historical event recall through an event hierarchy, and ReMem combines recursively compressed context with question-conditioned visual reinjection. This revises the earlier historical EDMI description: training-free operation, use of an existing state or a different trigger could distinguish a precise variant, but changing entropy or attention thresholds does not establish originality. A narrower retrieval decision would need calibrated benefit, false-trigger analysis and overhead-matched controls. ([StreamFlow primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10949v1), [local PDF, §3.4 pp5–6](../related_papers/2608.10949v1.pdf), [OASIS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Liang_OASIS_On-Demand_Hierarchical_Event_Memory_for_Streaming_Video_Reasoning_CVPR_2026_paper.html), [ReMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05940))

**Delayed evidence, object memory and compressed summaries also have close comparators.** ProtoKV keeps exact near context and bounded prototype/residual state for distant history, and evaluates delayed retrospective queries. ObjectStream retains latent object histories and transient changes; MEMO uses entity-aware indices and a CPU visual-evidence pool. FreshMem explicitly balances frequency-space gist and residual spatial detail. Thus generic object awareness, persistent history, summary tokens or delayed-query motivation cannot carry the new claim. The meaningful residual questions concern actual decisive-cue age, identity errors, absence evidence and information lost through repeated consolidation. ([ProtoKV](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/minh26a.html), [ObjectStream](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28312), [MEMO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38900), [FreshMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01683v2))

The newest abstract checks constrain cheap scoring proposals further. LORE-KV samples future continuations and uses response-side query states with deletion-cost estimates and optional trajectory weighting. KV² uses a cheap proxy to choose context tokens for selective reconstruction and eviction scoring. Video-MemComp implements pre-RoPE aggregation with attention-conserving bias and sensitivity-driven layer budgets. DeltaS reads gated-linear-attention state drift as a retention signal and already compares signals under a fixed policy and budget. These findings establish implemented conceptual overlap. They do not settle whether a particular persistent, history-conditioned video probe state and its invalidation criterion are new; that requires the nearest full equations, code and later versions. ([LORE-KV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07643v2), [KV²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03198v2), [Video-MemComp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32784v1), [DeltaS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27470v1))

## Evidence survival, feedback and complete cost define the useful gap families

The first family is **failure attribution and evidence age**. HERMES and FIFO share most errors, so replacing retention is useful only where the necessary visual information was observed and could support a correct answer. The diagnostic sequence is to determine whether a cue was sampled, whether the backbone can interpret it, whether it entered memory, whether it survived eviction and whether the answer uses it correctly. VILA, InternVL, LLaVA-OneVision and the other backbone papers describe capability improvements, not guarantees that a small frozen model can answer from retained evidence. StreamingTOM explicitly notes perception and long-range reasoning limits (PDF p14), and CausalMem gives similar frozen-backbone caveats (Appendix A, PDF p13). Oracle-support input is a diagnostic control, not a deployable memory method. ([StreamingTOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18269v2), [CausalMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25658), [LLaVA-OneVision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03326))

Evidence age must be measured from the support, not assumed from video length or query time. EgoSchema's temporal certificates already make this distinction conceptually. VideoLLaMB's NIAVH tests vary needle depth and length (§3.6, PDF p6), so controlled video needles are existing evaluation. ProtoKV explicitly lacks ground-truth last-evidence timestamps and measures delay from the dataset query timestamp (§4.1, PDF p6). A useful extension would annotate oldest and latest necessary evidence, guarantee visibility at the sampling rate and test natural distributed support as well as localized cues. Retrospective answers must remain valid when a question is delayed; present-state questions can change their correct answer. ([EgoSchema](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09126), [VideoLLaMB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01071), [ProtoKV local PDF, §4.1 p6](../related_papers/minh26a.pdf))

Within that family, rare static text, transient identity information and jointly necessary events deserve separate strata. Novelty, motion, curvature and salience do not logically guarantee future-question relevance. That is an analyst hypothesis about their signals, not a demonstrated failure of FluxMem, CurveStream, CausalMem or FreshMem. Repeated-compression experiments should hold the original cue, answer and total memory fixed while increasing intervening irrelevant chunks and consolidation count. ReMem already targets long-context retention, and ProtoKV already supplies summary-state memory; the potential contribution must explain a particular failure and repair rather than merely repeat the motivation. ([ReMem methods, PDF pp3–4](../related_papers/2610.05940v1.pdf), [FreshMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01683v2))

The second family is **scoring fidelity and state freshness**. The local manual scorer freezes pseudo-query embeddings across layers and omits a causal mask. On one 16-frame prefix, mean cosine against native attention is 0.5593 and retained-token Jaccard is 0.6503 at a constraining 256-token budget. This demonstrates numerical and selection differences on that prefix; corrected-scorer QA has not been measured. Restoring causal propagation, positions and compaction alignment is a reproduction correction. Video-CCAM's causal projector is a different operation, while DSCache and Video-MemComp already address aspects of pre-RoPE memory construction. None of those facts turns fixing this local path into an original algorithm. ([Project status](../PROJECT_STATUS.md), [local scorer](../../inference/llavaov_hermes.py), [Video-CCAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14023), [DSCache](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/pang26a.html))

A conditional method question follows only if a fresh causal reference improves useful behavior: can layer-wise propagated probe states be reused across ingestion updates and refreshed when cache evolution makes them unreliable? The operational distinction would be contextual-state reuse and invalidation during continual unknown-question video compression, compared with a static semantic bank, fresh scoring and selective reconstruction. Text changes after a question, retained-token identity, eviction and RoPE alignment all affect validity. A scene-change threshold or fixed refresh schedule is a control, not sufficient originality. All cached state and trigger computation belong in the budget; attention agreement alone is not an answer-quality guarantee. SAVEMem, FlexMem/MemIndex, KV² and LORE-KV are mandatory comparison obligations before developing the claim. ([SAVEMem pp4–5](../related_papers/2605.07897v1.pdf), [FlexMem §3.3.2, PDF p5](../related_papers/2603.29252v1.pdf), [online overlap records](<../research_notes/HERMES research gap map/online_updates.json>))

The third family is **error feedback through historical conditioning**. The local `predict_next_question` path reads a previous generated answer, converts option letters into option text and includes that history in local and global guidance prompts. Thus an incorrect answer can change later retention decisions. Actual harm remains untested. D-HSM explicitly admits generated observation errors propagating into textual memory (§7, PDF p10); MEMO admits detector, segmenter and association error propagation (§5, PDF p8). These are supporting analogies with different error sources. LORE-KV's reliability weighting concerns sampled future trajectories. No globally novel past-answer correction claim follows from those comparisons. SAVEMem and StreamAgent need a focused feedback-policy check before claiming originality; full-text clearance against StreamAgent was not completed in this review. ([Local dependency](../../inference/llavaov_hermes.py), [D-HSM §7 p10](../related_papers/2608.30294v1.pdf), [MEMO §5 p8](../related_papers/2609.38900v1.pdf))

A controlled diagnostic can fork the same cache and vary only historical answer conditioning: original generated answer, corrected past answer, plausible wrong past answer and question-only history. Corrected history can use a past ground-truth answer for diagnosis; it cannot use future answers. Freeze subsequent frames and questions. Measure selection divergence, supporting-cue survival and next-answer changes. Only if a repeatable error-mediated effect appears should question-only conditioning, confidence gating or refresh be considered as remedies. A changed token set without downstream harm does not justify a new mechanism.

The fourth family concerns **absence, identity and state transitions**. MEMO explicitly identifies absence-based/implicit states, persistent cross-chunk identity and long-horizon consolidation as limitations (PDF p8). KeyRec lacks explicit identity/trajectory/state modeling (Appendix E, PDF p17), but ObjectStream already stores object evolution and transient changes. A narrower test must distinguish an object leaving from occlusion, failed detection and genuine absence, and measure false absence and identity switches at equal bytes. Hour/day consolidation is a valid unresolved direction in the source, but a poor first project under a few rented GPU days. Annotation and tracking reliability could dominate the effort before a retention effect is isolated. ([MEMO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38900), [KeyRec Appendix E p17](../related_papers/2609.32182v1.pdf), [ObjectStream](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28312))

The fifth family is **complete memory and operational streaming cost**. Active GPU KV, CPU/disk evidence, indices, embeddings, prototypes, query states and transfer buffers need separate growth curves. ReKV acknowledges unsustainable offloaded growth (PDF p16), MemStream reports stored-cache growth, and MEMO's separate CPU pool differs from strictly bounded total memory. A retrieval method with expanding history and a strict-eviction method solve different resource problems. Query-agnostic ingestion should also be separated from query-time retrieval or reconstruction, including first-query and repeated-query latency. StreamKV observes that more retrieved frames can reduce accuracy (PDF p7); MemStream reports task-dependent retrieval regressions (PDF p7). More recall is not automatically better holistic reasoning. ([ReKV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00540v1), [MemStream](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18434v1), [StreamKV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07278v1))

The local timings leave approximately 93% of video wall time outside encoding, compression and answer timers. CPU video decoding is a candidate component, but its causal contribution has not been isolated. The harness loads video into CPU memory, then sends timestamp-limited prefixes to the model: causal model input does not by itself establish live ingestion performance. SAVEMem explicitly calls its evaluation pseudo-streaming without encoding-latency penalties (Appendix A.3, PDF p15). KeyRec reports an attempted encoder reduction that disrupted the optimized path without wall-time gains (PDF p17). STC already caches adjacent-frame encoder features. Profiling must precede an optimization; token savings alone cannot support a whole-pipeline speed claim. ([Timing scope](../PROJECT_STATUS.md), [loading](../../video_qa/base.py), [prefix loop](../../video_qa/hermes_vqa.py), [SAVEMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07897v1), [STC](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Wang_Accelerating_Streaming_Video_Large_Language_Models_via_Hierarchical_Token_Compression_CVPR_2026_paper.html))

## A small pilot should choose the method before the large run

The practical priority is a CPU-supported evidence audit, followed by an inference-only reference comparison. Select roughly 20–30 existing discordant/shared-failure examples for diagnosis and annotate support windows, sampled visibility, modality, dialogue dependence and error type. Outcome-selected examples cannot estimate unbiased benchmark accuracy. Check a few oracle-support cases with the present backbone and, if feasible, an available stronger checkpoint. This identifies whether retention has recoverable headroom. Audio-dependent, fine-detail and reasoning failures belong in their own categories. Existing MovieNet, Ego4D, Video-MME, MVBench, StreamingBench and OVO protocols already cover many relevant settings; using them is evaluation infrastructure, not a new task claim. ([Early corpus benchmark notes](<../research_notes/HERMES research gap map/corpus_early.md>), [OVO limitations §9, PDF p13](../related_papers/2501.05510v2.pdf))

Then run two or three representative videos to establish native-reference feasibility, memory and real throughput. On a fixed development subset compare FIFO, current HERMES and fresh causal scoring under identical frames, questions, precision, position handling and memory. **Fresh native causal attention scoring uses the same compressed cache; it is distinct from native full-prefix backbone QA**, which needs a separate feasible-context subset. The existing HERMES 50K condition does not supply that native QA baseline. Question-only and recent-window controls can cheaply test whether the diagnostic workload needs distant visual memory. Record paired answer outcomes, cue survival, compression/update time, total wall time and all auxiliary bytes. Continue toward state reuse only if the fresh reference produces promising evidence recovery or QA behavior at tolerable cost. If it merely changes attention or selected sets, revisit attribution before adding complexity. The 50-video component ablations all have intervals including zero, so their present rankings should not determine a complicated new policy. ([Current measured limits](../PROJECT_STATUS.md))

If reuse passes that gate, compare fresh scoring, reuse without refresh, fixed-interval refresh, scene-change refresh, random refresh with matched count and one predeclared selective rule. The central question is whether the rule improves the measured QA/cost trade-off beyond schedules receiving the same refresh budget. Use stable token identities, explicit history invalidation and native position semantics. Separately, the history-conditioning intervention is a cheap diagnostic on a few cases; it should not be bundled with refresh initially, because that would obscure which change produced the result. Neither pilot is a request to train a new compressor, memory bridge or test-time-training model.

Other affordable options depend on the observed failure. If decisive evidence was never sampled, investigate sampling/retention allocation at equal encoded-frame cost rather than improving an eviction score. If existing retrieval adds irrelevant history, calibrate readout against no/always/selective retrieval and account for its latency. If profiling identifies front-end work as dominant, test an existing compatible feature-reuse control before claiming a new acceleration. These are conditional branches retained in the register; none currently has demonstrated local gains or cleared novelty.

All StreamingBench results have already been inspected and influenced the research direction. New tuning there is development reuse. Freeze the mechanism and comparisons before confirmation, disclose reused videos and mixed sessions, and seek a separate untouched benchmark or prospectively reserved evaluation where possible. Audit Ego4D-derived source-video overlap before treating two benchmarks as independent domain evidence. Use paired video-cluster uncertainty, disclose subgroup exploration and keep judge/parser versions fixed. Preserve negative and inconclusive findings. GPU availability and measured pilot throughput should determine actual run counts; a few days alone cannot justify a fabricated runtime forecast. ([Project evidence boundaries](../PROJECT_STATUS.md), [Ego4D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07058), [MuKV judge caveat, §4.1 PDF p5](../related_papers/Xiao_MuKV_Multi-Grained_KV_Cache_Compression_for_Long_Streaming_Video_Question-Answering_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf))

The professor's original-method requirement means a faithful reproduction correction or stronger evaluation cannot alone supply the intended contribution. The promising path is to identify a precise recoverable failure, derive a distinct intervention, and show why its benefit survives matched controls. **Causal-state reuse, error-sensitive conditioning and absence-aware evidence are conditional opportunities, not proven novelty.** The register keeps their evidence and rejection conditions explicit, allowing the next compute allocation to buy a justified research decision before a broad campaign. No GPU experiment was conducted for this review.
